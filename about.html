What is this?

Hi. Once upon a time, I was a magnificent menace of a student, yet entirely obsessed with tearing technology apart to see its guts. Now? I’m older, objectively dumber, and deeply regretful that never had a straightforward way to actually talk to the professionals doing the real work around town. So...

Get the following metaphore:

"If you could revive Vivaldi to ask him — 'but how do you do that X, Y and Z thingy on the violin?' Now you get straight discrete answer in 5 minutes rather than spending weeks to months emulating his sound."
Please have a slight disregard to any wobbles of mine. I’ve been having a few drinks.

I belive we all adore the creative part of engineering as a recreative and inqisitive matter, and for me whats getting the fire going is learning about tools, technologies and applicaitons. So much so the main trend in discussions I catch when talking with developers is always around the toolbelt. - So could you point where virtually are these discussions held at TU-Plovdiv? I wish I could tell.


Another thing - What about reserach?
Wouldn't it be cool to have a simple acccessable blog or journal where a student can get a feel what are the clever heads actually doing? All the years spend here and it's just now I'm enlighened things. I've been around enough to tell that there are entusiats that would like to be outsourced with some trivial work on analysis, articles and developments.


One thing westen and leading European universities do is organazing Hackatons with the soul purpose of serarching for talant and allowing people to get a "remote" feel of what kind of problems does a X company solves, what you can learn from them and perhaps getting a good estimate how much you need to 'know' before entertaining the idea of working in the field.

So, I’m putting the queries to you. Would you like a digital campfire?
  • Domains of research: overview of active developments and objectives
  • Blog — academic notes, project updates, tool reveiws and events
  • Homebrew — showcase of local open-source projects
  • PhD student profiles and active research topics
  • IRC room — live, open-access channel for discussion
  • Contact form routing directly to the department

This website was forged in the fires of sheer, unyielding stubbornness. No trendy frameworks, no bundlers, no transpilers that will be obsolete in three weeks. Every page is naked HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The backend is a self-hosted NixOS box sitting in the dark behind a Cloudflare Tunnel. The source is open, the dependencies are practically zero, and the madness is documented below.

Innerworkings

A full technical breakdown. All components are open-source or standard platform services. Nothing proprietary.

$ cat README.md

# ctrl-sys-site
HTML5 CSS3 Vanilla JS Node.js 22 SQLite Ergo IRCd NixOS 25.11 Netlify Cloudflare open source
Frontend
  • HTML5 — semantic markup, no templates or preprocessors
  • CSS3 — ~3200-line master sheet, 8 named themes, CSS variable system
  • Vanilla JS — no frameworks, no bundlers, no transpilers
  • grid-fx.js — 14+ cycling canvas visualisation modes
  • DOMPurify v3.2 — blog markdown sanitisation
  • Tabler Icons — SVG icon library via CDN
  • Leaflet.js — campus map, contact section
Backend
PackagePurpose
Node.js 22 + ExpressHTTP runtime and API framework
better-sqlite3Synchronous SQLite — post storage, zero-config
multerMultipart file uploads — images · PDFs · archives (50 MB cap)
express-rate-limitRate limiting — 5 req/h subscribe, 30 req/15 min admin
node:crypto AES-256-GCMSubscriber email encryption at rest
morgan · dotenv · corsDev logging, environment, cross-origin policy
IRC
  • Ergo IRCd — single Go binary, no external dependencies
  • Channel — #control-systems · open guest access · 50-message history
  • Client — irc.js, vanilla WebSocket, no IRC library
  • Nick persistence — localStorage, 7-day TTL
Hosting & Deployment
  • Netlify — static frontend + Functions (contact form → Discord webhook)
  • Self-hosted — NixOS machine, backend via systemd
  • Cloudflare Tunnel — cloudflared → public HTTPS/WSS, no open inbound ports
OS / Infra
  • NixOS 25.11 — declarative, reproducible, flake.nix pinned inputs
  • systemd units — ddl-backend · ddl-cloudflared · ddl-ergo
  • XRDP over LAN — XFCE desktop for maintenance access
